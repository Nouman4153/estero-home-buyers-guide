Buying a Home on the Corkscrew Road Corridor in Estero, Florida

Estero keeps growing, and much of that growth runs along one road. The Corkscrew Road corridor, east of I-75, is where new communities, new construction and established neighbourhoods meet. If you are buying here, the street you choose matters as much as the house itself, because each community has its own builder, HOA and fees.

Start with the community, not the listing

A good first step is to narrow down two or three communities before you start touring homes. Ask about the HOA, the CDD, what the fees actually cover, and how far the community is from everyday shopping and schools. A home that looks perfect on paper can feel very different once you drive the route at rush hour.

New construction: Kingston and the corridor

One of the biggest stories on the corridor is Kingston, a Cameratta Companies development of more than 7,600 acres east of I-75, with over 3,750 acres set aside as preserve. Model homes opened in October 2026 with Lennar, Pulte and Neal Communities, and homes start in the $330s. John Colin & Co. is registered with all five Kingston builders, and your own representation on new construction costs you nothing as the buyer.

That last point is worth knowing before you walk into a model home alone. The builder's sales team works for the builder. Having your own agent means someone checks incentives, lot premiums and release timing on your side.

What to check before you make an offer

  • True comparable sales in the same community, not just the same town.
  • HOA and CDD costs, and what they cover.
  • Whether the home is in a new release, where incentives change fast.
  • Resale history on the street, if the home is not new.
